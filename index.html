1 · The problem

The gold standard is a committee of physicians, reading.

1.1 How committees review events

A cardiovascular outcome trial is only as reliable as its event count. When a site reports a possible heart-failure hospitalization or myocardial infarction, the patient's records are collected, redacted and translated into a dossier. Two physicians on a central clinical events committee review it independently against standardized, FDA-endorsed endpoint definitions. If they disagree, the case goes to a committee meeting and the chair breaks the tie.

The process is rigorous, and it is the reason trial endpoints are trusted. It is also manual, and its cost grows with every event the trial accrues.

1.2 Limits of committee review

Slow

Trial closeout waits until the last dossier has been read twice and every disagreement has been discussed in committee.

Costly

Trials supporting approval of new cardiovascular drugs cost more than $35,000 per participant. Physician review is one of the costs that scales with event count.

Imperfectly reproducible

Two reviewers on one committee agree at κ 0.72 on heart-failure hospitalization. Two different committees agree at κ 0.60.

Hard to audit

A verdict is recorded as an outcome. The reasoning that produced it is rarely captured in a form a regulator or sponsor can check.

2 · How it works

Two independent readers, as in a committee.

We did not try to replace the committee's judgment with a single model. We rebuilt its structure. Each dossier is read by two AI systems that work in different ways.

2.1 Two complementary readers

Reader 1 · reasoning language model

Applies the definition and quotes the record

Given the FDA / SCTI endpoint definition and the dossier text, it checks each criterion, quotes the sentences that satisfy it, and explains its decision.

Reader 2 · trained classifier

Has learned from committee decisions

A Clinical Longformer model fine-tuned on thousands of events adjudicated by physician committees in completed trials. It scores how likely a committee would be to confirm the event.

When both readers agree with confidence, the decision stands with its evidence attached. When they disagree, the event is flagged as uncertain and routed to a human adjudicator. Disagreement between independent readers is also how a committee finds its hard cases.

2.2 Every decision shows its work.

For each criterion in the definition (primary diagnosis, length of stay, symptoms, objective signs, treatment) the model quotes the sentence in the record that satisfies it. A reviewer can check a decision by reading five highlighted lines instead of forty pages. Across DELIVER, the model documented dyspnea in 86% of dossiers, peripheral edema in 66% and intravenous loop diuretics in 78%.

3 · The evidence

Validated where adjudication is hardest.

The models were trained on committee-adjudicated events from completed trials and tested on trials they had never seen. Both test trials were global, with translated records of uneven quality from many countries. Sites submitted only suspected events, so obvious non-events never reached the committee or the models. What remained were the hard cases.

3.2 Confidence tracks the committee.

Combining the two readers gives every event a score from −2 (confident not HF) to +2 (confident HF). The higher the score, the more often the physician committee had confirmed the event: 17% at the bottom of the scale, 96% at the top. The middle of the scale is where the work is, and the model knows it.

3.4 The same approach extends to MACE.

In PARADISE-MI, Auto-MACE applied the two-reader design to cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction and stroke. Where the readers agreed, the AI matched the committee 88–97% of the time. Sending only the uncertain events to physicians raised agreement to 89–98%.

With no physician review at all, agreement was 86% for deaths, 76% for MIs and 84% for strokes. Text-extraction problems with lab tables and checkbox forms contributed to MI errors.

3.5 The trial result does not move.

The question a sponsor cares about is whether AI adjudication would change the trial's answer. Re-running both primary analyses with AI-adjudicated events gave nearly identical treatment-effect estimates.

4 · Anjana

A cockpit for the people who adjudicate.

4.1 The adjudicator workflow

Anjana turns the research into a working tool for clinical events committees. Adjudicators keep the final word. They open a queue the AI has already read, see the source dossier beside the AI's recommendation, check the evidence for and against each criterion, then confirm or override and sign.

  1. Queue

    Every case arrives pre-read

    Each event in the adjudicator's queue carries the AI's recommendation. Uncertain cases stand out.

  2. Cockpit

    Evidence beside the source

    The redacted dossier sits next to the recommendation, and each criterion links to the sentences for and against it.

  3. Decision

    The adjudicator signs

    Confirm or override, complete the committee's form, sign, and move to the next case.

4.2 Where it applies.

Pivotal trials keep their committees and spend physician time only where it matters. Pragmatic trials that enroll whole health systems or countries cannot afford chart review at all and fall back on diagnosis codes; automated adjudication can bring committee-grade endpoints to studies that never had them.

4.3 What comes next.

  • Prospective use

    Our results re-adjudicate completed trials. Running the hybrid workflow live will measure real savings in time and cost.

  • Beyond text

    Lab tables and ECG images were not read by the models. Multimodal inputs should most improve myocardial-infarction adjudication.

  • Untranslated records

    Dossiers were translated by humans before review. Reading source-language records directly would remove a costly step.

  • Upstream steps

    Event identification, record collection and date confirmation are still manual and are natural next targets.

  • Regulatory pathway

    A validation framework agreed with the FDA and EMA is the route to AI-adjudicated endpoints in pivotal trials.

5 · Why now

AI is filling the pipeline.
Every outcome trial still ends in adjudication.

5.1 AI-discovered drugs are reaching patients, fast.

Drug discovery is changing faster than drug development. In 2022, an analysis of 20 AI-native biotechnology companies counted about 15 AI-derived assets in clinical development, from a pipeline growing about 36% a year. [1] By December 2025, an industry-wide census counted 117 AI-enabled drugs, from 63 companies, that had entered interventional trials. [2]

The early molecules are surviving. In the first analysis of their clinical record, 80–90% of AI-discovered molecules succeeded in Phase I, against 52% for the industry across 2011–2020. [3] [4] Rentosertib shows the pace. Generative AI found both its target and its molecule, and it reached a preclinical candidate in about 18 months. [5] It completed a randomized Phase IIa in 2025 and began a 320-patient Phase III in July 2026. [6] [7]

5.2 More drugs in, more events to adjudicate.

AI compresses discovery, but not the evidence a drug needs to reach patients. Molecules that survive early trials move into outcome trials, the largest studies in drug development. Cardiovascular outcome trials commonly enroll tens of thousands of participants. Ten of them together accrued more than 9,000 events among 95,038 patients. [8] A committee of physicians reads every one of those events.

That reading already carries a measurable cost. Across nine stroke trials, adjudication cost more than £100,000 in a third of them, about £2,300 for each event the committee corrected. [9] Meanwhile, the route to the clinic is getting shorter. In April 2025, the FDA announced a plan to make animal testing the exception, naming AI-based computational models of toxicity among the replacements. [10] Capital is following: almost $10 billion in AI and machine-learning deals were announced in 2024. [11]

Our thesis: AI multiplies the molecules entering trials, and each one still needs its outcomes adjudicated. Anjana sits at that bottleneck. Its market grows with the number of outcome trials and with the number of events in each.

What the evidence does not yet show: AI-discovered drugs have not outperformed in Phase II, and none has completed Phase III. The case rests on the volume of drugs entering the clinic, not on higher late-stage success.

Cardiovascular trials are where we start, not where the market ends. When a trial's result depends on clinical events, sponsors pay physicians to adjudicate them. Large published trials in lung, kidney and metabolic disease already do.

6.2 A close variant of the same tool.

Anjana's first reader receives the endpoint definition as text, alongside the dossier. A new endpoint therefore starts as a new definition, not a new model. The same approach has already moved from heart-failure hospitalization in DELIVER to cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction and stroke in PARADISE-MI (section 3.4).

Dossier intake, criterion-level evidence, uncertainty routing and physician sign-off carry over unchanged. Two things change for each specialty: the definitions, and the second reader, which is retrained on committee-adjudicated events from that field. Each new endpoint is validated against specialist committees before it is used. For investors, each specialty adds outcome trials to the same product without a new platform.

  1. Define

    Write the endpoint

    Agree the clinical criteria with specialists, and identify the text, laboratory results and images each decision needs.

  2. Retrain and validate

    Test against the committee

    Retrain the classifier on adjudicated events from the specialty. Compare with committee decisions on trials the model has never seen.

  3. Deploy

    Start with human oversight

    Measure agreement, reviewer time and cost in live trials, with physicians signing every decision.

7 · The lab

Built where the trials are adjudicated.

Anjana grew out of the Cardiovascular Division at Brigham and Women's Hospital, part of Mass General Brigham, and Harvard Medical School. The group led by Scott Solomon runs the hospital's Cardiac Imaging Core Laboratory and Clinical Trials Endpoints Center, and its physician committees have adjudicated outcomes for large global cardiovascular trials. Those committee decisions are what Anjana's models learned from.
